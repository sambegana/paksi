## 1. Core legal framework 2008–2019: instruments, obligations, sanctions (UU 18/2008, PP 81/2012, PP 27/2020, Perpres 97/2017, P.75/2019, Perpres 35/2018, Perpres 83/2018, regional bag bans)

### Takeaway
UU 18/2008 puts *formal* obligations on all three actors: the state (guarantee and fund services), every person (reduce and handle household waste), and producers (label and manage packaging that is hard to decompose, Pasal 15). But the only part with a hard, enforceable timetable and criminal sanctions sits with waste **operators**, the end of the chain (open-dumping ban, Pasal 29, 40, 44). Producer duties were turned into a 10-year "roadmap" (P.75/2019) that is weakly enforced. None of the 2025 national targets (Jakstranas 30/70, marine plastic -70%) were met.

### Cited Findings

**UU No. 18 Tahun 2008 tentang Pengelolaan Sampah** (promulgated 7 May 2008; still in force) [SECONDARY, via pasal.id consolidated text]
- Scope: household waste, waste similar to household waste, and specific waste (*sampah spesifik*) (Pasal 2). Principles include *tanggung jawab* (responsibility) and *nilai ekonomi* (economic value) (Pasal 3) — [pasal.id UU 18/2008](https://pasal.id/peraturan/uu/uu-no-18-tahun-2008)
- **Government:** the central and regional governments must guarantee sound waste management and fund it; authority is split between levels (Pasal 5–10, 24) — [pasal.id](https://pasal.id/peraturan/uu/uu-no-18-tahun-2008)
- **Every person:** must reduce and handle household waste in an environmentally sound way (Pasal 12) — [pasal.id](https://pasal.id/peraturan/uu/uu-no-18-tahun-2008)
- **Area managers** (housing estates, commercial and industrial zones, public and social facilities): must provide waste-sorting facilities (Pasal 13), with a one-year transition (Pasal 45) — [pasal.id](https://pasal.id/peraturan/uu/uu-no-18-tahun-2008)
- **Producers:** must put labels on products about waste reduction and handling (Pasal 14), and must manage packaging and/or goods they produce that cannot be, or are hard to be, decomposed by natural processes (Pasal 15) — [pasal.id](https://pasal.id/peraturan/uu/uu-no-18-tahun-2008)
- Reduction means limiting waste generation, recycling, and reuse (Pasal 20). Government gives incentives for reduction and disincentives to those who do not reduce (Pasal 21) — [pasal.id](https://pasal.id/peraturan/uu/uu-no-18-tahun-2008)
- **Prohibitions (Pasal 29)** include importing waste, mixing waste with B3, littering, open dumping at final disposal sites, and burning waste that does not meet technical standards — [pasal.id](https://pasal.id/peraturan/uu/uu-no-18-tahun-2008)
- **Criminal sanctions:** illegal import 3–9 years (household waste) or 4–12 years (specific waste) (Pasal 39); an operator who knowingly manages waste without meeting standards, 4–10 years (Pasal 40); negligence causing disturbance or pollution, up to 3 years (Pasal 41); corporate liability (Pasal 42) — [pasal.id](https://pasal.id/peraturan/uu/uu-no-18-tahun-2008)
- **Open-dumping deadline (Pasal 44):** open-dumping TPA must have closure plans within 1 year and be closed within 5 years of the law taking effect. That means by about 2013. — [pasal.id](https://pasal.id/peraturan/uu/uu-no-18-tahun-2008)
- **Lawsuits:** tort claims (PMH), class actions, and suits by qualifying waste organisations are possible (Pasal 33–37) — [pasal.id](https://pasal.id/peraturan/uu/uu-no-18-tahun-2008)
- Note: UU 18/2008 has **no explicit criminal or administrative sanction for a producer that breaches Pasal 15.** The Pasal 39–42 penalties target importing, operators, and polluters. This is an inference from the summary of Pasal 39–42 above; check against the full official text.

### Inferences
- The framework gives the most *specific* duties to waste operators and regional governments (Pasal 29 and 44 plus criminal penalties), the *broadest* duty to every person (Pasal 12), and only a *general and largely unenforced* duty to producers (Pasal 14–15). P.75/2019 turned Pasal 15 into a 10-year voluntary-in-practice exercise. Seven years in, 28 producers report implementation.
- Regional bag bans put the compliance burden on retailers and venue managers. Fast-moving consumer goods (FMCG) packaging producers upstream were largely untouched, except through the 2030 polystyrene/PVC phase-out in P.75.
- The 2013 deadline for closing open dumps in UU 18/2008 was missed by more than a decade (see section 4). This failure belongs to the state and regional governments, not producers or households.

### Gaps
- Exact text of P.75/2019 on sanctions (its own Pasal on sanctions/disincentives) was not fetched. Check against peraturan.bpk.go.id or JDIH.
- PP 81/2012 Pasal 12–15 and PP 27/2020 contents were not verified this session.
- Official final 2025 Jakstranas achievement (year-end SIPSN) was not found. Only the April 2025 snapshot and contradictory 2025–2026 ministerial figures are available (see section 2).
- Bogor Perwali 61/2018, other regional bans (e.g., Banjarmasin, Balikpapan), and their enforcement were not verified.
- Perpres 35/2018 tipping-fee and tariff terms were not verified in this session.

## 2. Newest developments 2024–2026: institutions, PSEL Perpres, open-dumping closures, EPR revision, RPJMN targets, plastic excise, import rules

### Takeaway
Under Prabowo, environmental affairs moved to a new Kementerian Lingkungan Hidup and a separate BPLH (Perpres 182 and 183/2024). The state's response has been mostly *downstream and state- or SOE-led*. Perpres 109/2025 created a waste-to-energy scheme backed by Danantara, with a fixed 20 US cents/kWh tariff that PLN must pay for 30 years. KLH also ran an enforcement campaign against regional governments using open-dumping TPA, with a 31 July 2026 deadline. Producer EPR is still a draft replacement of P.75 (expected late 2026, with full obligations only from 2028). Plastic excise has been postponed every year since it was first proposed.

**Perpres No. 109 Tahun 2025 (Penanganan Sampah Perkotaan melalui Pengolahan Sampah menjadi Energi Terbarukan Berbasis Teknologi Ramah Lingkungan / PSEL)** — official PDF: [peraturan.go.id](https://peraturan.go.id/files/perpres-no-109-tahun-2025.pdf); KLH announcement: [kemenlh.go.id](https://kemenlh.go.id/news/detail/presiden-tetapkan-perpres-1092025-langkah-nyata-atasi-sampah-perkotaan)

- **Tariff (Pasal 19):** PLN must buy all PSEL electricity at **USD 0.20/kWh** for all capacities. The tariff is final, with no negotiation and no escalation, for **30 years** from commercial operation. PSEL gets priority grid access. If this raises PLN's generation cost (BPP), **the government compensates PLN**, including the cost of grid connection to the nearest substation (Pasal 19–20) — [Justisio](https://justisio.com/blog/tinjau-perpres-109-2025); [ANTARA](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/5538365/pemerintah-terbitkan-perpres-109-2025-percepat-pembangunan-psel); [Ecobiz Asia](https://ecobiz.asia/perpres-109-2025-tarif-listrik-dari-sampah-tetap-pln-wajib-beli/). This possibly conflicts with "final"; verify.
- **Danantara (Pasal 5, 14, 15):** BPI Danantara carries out technical and economic studies, selects the developer and operator (BUPP PSEL), or invests directly. Direct appointment is allowed in a waste emergency or when only one bidder qualifies — [Justisio](https://justisio.com/blog/tinjau-perpres-109-2025)
- **Regional government obligations (Pasal 4, 8–13):** guarantee at least **1,000 tons/day** of waste supply; budget APBD funds for collection and transport; commit to a regional *retribusi* (waste levy) regulation; and lend land to the developer **free of charge** — [Justisio](https://justisio.com/blog/tinjau-perpres-109-2025); [ANTARA](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/5538365/pemerintah-terbitkan-perpres-109-2025-percepat-pembangunan-psel)
- Environmental approval (Amdal) is capped at **2 months, with automatic approval** if the deadline passes (Pasal 18). Fiscal incentives include a VAT exemption (Pasal 22, 26) — [Justisio](https://justisio.com/blog/tinjau-perpres-109-2025). ANTARA reports environmental permitting cut from 12–24 months to two months — [ANTARA](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/5538365/pemerintah-terbitkan-perpres-109-2025-percepat-pembangunan-psel)
- **Pipeline (April 2026):** 30 locations or agglomerations covering 61 regencies/cities, about 33,000 t/day in total. Groundbreaking was targeted for June 2026 at Kota Bekasi, Kota Yogyakarta, Bogor Raya, Denpasar Raya, and Bandung Raya — [ANTARA, 22 Apr 2026](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/5538365/pemerintah-terbitkan-perpres-109-2025-percepat-pembangunan-psel). Danantara tendering was reported to start in November 2025 — [search summary](https://faktariau.id/2025/10/15/kepastian-30-tahun-perpres-pltsa-nomor-109-tahun-2025-beri-harga-final-20-sen-kwh/)

**EPR: replacing P.75/2019** [VERIFIED-OFFICIAL + SECONDARY]
- 8 Sep 2026: KLH/BPLH is "drafting a revision" to clarify EPR. Producers would pay **fees to a Producer Responsibility Organization (PRO)**, a non-profit set up by producers, in proportion to packaging volume and type, or would run take-back. The PRO works with regional governments and recyclers. Sectors: FMCG, AMDK (bottled water), retail, and F&B manufacturing. **Importers** are newly responsible for the packaging they import. Based on the polluter-pays principle. (The press release mistakenly refers to "Permen LHK 75/2018".) — [KLH, 8 Sep 2026](https://kemenlh.go.id/news/detail/kejar-target-pengelolaan-sampah-klhbplh-siapkan-peta-jalan-dan-perbarui-aturan-tanggung-jawab-produsen)
- 6–7 Oct 2026 public forum: Deputy Minister Diaz Hendropriyono and Deputy Vinda Damayanti Ansjar described it as a **replacement, not a revision**. It adds a PRO and a PRO supervisory board, **administrative sanctions plus public disclosure** for non-compliant producers, and incentives for compliant ones. **Full producer obligations start in 2028**, as the basis for planning through 2037. Public consultation ran until 9 Oct 2026. **Enactment targeted for November 2026, with socialisation until May 2027** — [Ecobiz Asia, 7 Oct 2026](https://ecobiz.asia/kepatuhan-produsen-rendah-klh-rancang-ulang-permen-peta-jalan-pengelolaan-sampah/)

- Planned objects: plastic bags, multilayer plastic packaging, styrofoam, and plastic straws — [DDTC](https://news.ddtc.co.id/berita/nasional/1804161/catat-ini-4-produk-plastik-yang-diusulkan-kena-cukai)

- **Permendag No. 47/2025** (goods banned from import, enacted 29 Dec 2025) bans B3 waste and listed non-B3 waste — [Batamnews](https://www.batamnews.co.id/feed-124801-permendag-47-2025-resmi-berlaku-sejumlah-barang-dilarang-diimpor-termasuk-limbah-b3-hingga-barang-bekas.html)
- Policy statement: Minister Hanif said in late 2024 that there would be **no plastic scrap imports from 2025**, covering all types including sorted waste, by not issuing recommendations — [ANTARA Kalsel](https://kalsel.antaranews.com/berita/437281/menteri-lh-tidak-ada-lagi-impor-sampah-plastik-pada-2025); [Katadata](https://katadata.co.id/amp/ekonomi-hijau/ekonomi-sirkular/6729cddb89436/10-negara-pengirim-sampah-plastik-ke-indonesia-bakal-dilarang-mulai-2025/); [Jakarta Post, 23 Oct 2024](https://www.thejakartapost.com/indonesia/2024/10/23/new-environment-minister-to-evaluate-waste-import-policy)
- Critics note that import policy is split across three ministries (environment, industry, trade), so a ban announced by the environment ministry alone may leak — [GoodStats](https://goodstats.id/article/indonesia-terima-kiriman-sampah-dari-eropa-ada-plastik-hingga-kertas-Tpiyv)

### Inferences
- The 2024–2026 measures move the *financial* burden toward the **state budget and SOEs**: PLN buys at about twice its generation cost, the state compensates PLN, and Danantara invests. **Regional governments** must supply land for free and guarantee 1,000 t/day. Local levies (*retribusi*) push collection costs onto **households**. The design depends on a steady waste stream, which sits uneasily with waste-reduction goals.
- EPR remains the slowest track. Even if enacted in November 2026, full producer obligations begin only in 2028. That is about 20 years after UU 18/2008 Pasal 15.
- Official figures for waste "managed" fell from about 39% (2025, unverified data) to about 25% (2026, after counting open dumps as unmanaged). This shows earlier SIPSN figures overstated performance.

### Gaps
- Whether Perpres 109/2025 abolishes the regional tipping fee (BLPS) used under Perpres 35/2018 was not established; no source addressed it.
- Exact enactment date of Perpres 109/2025; whether any PSEL groundbreaking actually happened in June 2026.
- Whether the P.75 replacement was actually enacted in November 2026 (not yet found as of 6 Nov 2026).
- Plastic excise status in APBN 2026 and the 2027 budget: no clear source found.
- HS-code status of plastic scrap under Permendag 24/2025 as amended by 23/2026, and whether imports actually stopped in 2025 (trade data).

## 3. Indonesia's position in the Global Plastics Treaty negotiations (INC-5 Busan 2024, INC-5.2 Geneva 2025, later sessions)

### Takeaway
Indonesia **did not support production caps**. At INC-5.2 (Geneva, August 2025) its delegation head said Indonesia would not support restricting plastic production because this conflicts with the RPJMN. ## 4. Actual enforcement: court cases, administrative sanctions on regional governments (open dumping), citizen lawsuits

### Takeaway
Enforcement has been aimed mostly at **regional governments and TPA operators**. From 2025 KLH issued hundreds of administrative sanctions over open dumping, set a 31 July 2026 deadline, and opened criminal investigations at about 44 TPA. Courts have upheld regional plastic bans (Bali) and found government liable for river pollution (ECOTON's Brantas case, final in 2025). This session found **no enforcement action against a producer** under UU 18/2008 Pasal 15 or P.75/2019.

### Cited Findings

**Open-dumping enforcement against regional governments (2025–2026)**

## 5. Where the framework places the burden: producer vs government vs household/individual

### Takeaway
On paper, UU 18/2008 spreads duties across all three actors. In practice, the **operational and financial burden sits with government**, above all regional governments and, since 2025, the central budget, PLN, and Danantara through PSEL. A growing share is passed to **households** through sorting duties, retribusi levies, and retail bag bans. **Producers** carry the lightest enforced burden: a roadmap regime with 79 of 842 engaged producers submitting roadmaps and 28 reporting implementation. Binding EPR is planned only from 2028.

### Cited Findings
- **Producer:** UU 18/2008 Pasal 14–15 duties exist — [pasal.id](https://pasal.id/peraturan/uu/uu-no-18-tahun-2008). P.75/2019 compliance: 79 roadmaps submitted and 28 implementing, out of 842 producers engaged — [Ecobiz Asia, Oct 2026](https://ecobiz.asia/kepatuhan-produsen-rendah-klh-rancang-ulang-permen-peta-jalan-pengelolaan-sampah/). Reported producer reduction of about 242 kt (2025), against national generation of about 144,562 t/day (about 52.8 Mt/year by simple arithmetic) — [KLH, Sep 2026](https://kemenlh.go.id/news/detail/kejar-target-pengelolaan-sampah-klhbplh-siapkan-peta-jalan-dan-perbarui-aturan-tanggung-jawab-produsen). Plastic excise has been postponed repeatedly — [Tempo](https://www.tempo.co/ekonomi/kementerian-keuangan-nyatakan-cukai-plastik-tidak-berlaku-tahun-ini-1192761). The national treaty position rejects production caps — [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/indonesia-pushed-to-reject-plastic-treaty-latest-draft/)
- **Government (central):** Perpres 109/2025 makes the state compensate PLN for paying USD 0.20/kWh for 30 years, and gives Danantara the investment role — [Justisio](https://justisio.com/blog/tinjau-perpres-109-2025)
- **Government (regional):** under Perpres 109/2025, regional governments must provide free land, guarantee 1,000 t/day, budget APBD for transport, and adopt retribusi rules — [Justisio](https://justisio.com/blog/tinjau-perpres-109-2025). Regional governments face administrative and criminal enforcement over open dumping — [Kompas, Feb 2026](https://lestari.kompas.com/read/2026/02/25/161700486/klh-selidiki-44-tpa-yang-masih-open-dumping-pemda-terancam-dipidana)
- **Households and individuals:** UU 18/2008 Pasal 12 duty to reduce and handle household waste — [pasal.id](https://pasal.id/peraturan/uu/uu-no-18-tahun-2008). Retribusi levies are mandated as a condition for PSEL — [Justisio](https://justisio.com/blog/tinjau-perpres-109-2025). Bag bans change shopper behaviour, although legal liability sits with retailers and venue managers — [detik](https://finance.detik.com/industri/d-5061199/aturan-lengkap-larangan-belanja-pakai-kantong-plastik-di-dki)
- **Retail and businesses:** primary duty-holders under regional bag bans (Jakarta Pergub 142/2019; Bali Pergub 97/2018) — [Republika](https://news.republika.co.id/berita/q3w9d7349/pergub-kantong-ramah-lingkungan-bebani-pengelola-mal); [Mongabay](https://mongabay.co.id/2019/04/23/asosiasi-daur-ulang-ajukan-uji-materiil-pergub-larangan-plastik-sekali-pakai/)

### Inferences
- **Allocation summary:**

| Actor | Formal legal duty | Enforced in practice (2008–2026) | Financial burden |
|---|---|---|---|
| Producers/importers | Label (Pasal 14); manage hard-to-decompose packaging (Pasal 15); P.75 roadmap with 30% by 2029 | Very weak: 28 implementing; no documented sanction; EPR with PRO fees and sanctions only in draft, full obligations from 2028 | Minimal; excise never imposed |
| Central government | Guarantee and fund (Pasal 5–10); targets (Jakstranas, RPJMN 100% by 2029) | Acts as enforcer against regional governments; targets missed | Rising: PLN compensation, Danantara, incentives |
| Regional governments | Operate TPA; close open dumps (Pasal 44, by about 2013); retribusi | Most heavily enforced: 273–485 sanctions, criminal inquiries | High: APBD, free land, guaranteed waste supply |
| Households/citizens | Reduce and handle household waste (Pasal 12) | Indirect: levies, sorting, bag bans | Growing via retribusi |
| Retailers/area managers | Sorting facilities (Pasal 13); bag bans | Moderate (regional) | Moderate |

- Policy logic after 2024 is "downstream-first". Ending open dumping and building PSEL are framed as the route to 100% by 2029, while upstream instruments (EPR, excise, production limits) are deferred or rejected. This moves the cost of plastic packaging from producers to taxpayers, electricity ratepayers or the state budget, and households.
- PSEL's guaranteed 1,000 t/day supply and 30-year offtake create a structural interest in continued waste generation. This may conflict with the reduction hierarchy in UU 18/2008 Pasal 20. This is an analytical inference, not a sourced claim.

### Gaps
- No quantitative estimate of costs by actor (APBD vs APBN vs producer spending) was found.
- No ICEL or Bappenas analysis of burden allocation was retrieved in this session. ICEL papers on EPR and P.75 would strengthen this section.
- The household-level burden (retribusi rates, enforcement of sorting duties) was not quantified.

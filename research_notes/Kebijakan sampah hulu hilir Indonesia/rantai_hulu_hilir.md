> Researcher notes (compiled 6 Nov 2026). Budget-limited search (~15 tool calls). Every figure carries an inline source; most sources are secondary news reports that cite SIPSN/KLH, NGO reports or BPS. Figures marked **[estimate]** are modelled or come from a non-official source. ## Q1. Angka nasional timbulan, komposisi, dan % terkelola (SIPSN), plus masalah kualitas data

### Takeaway
SIPSN data for 2025 show about 24–29 million tonnes/year of reported waste, with only about 33–35% "terkelola" (managed). Food waste makes up about 39–40% and plastic about 19–20%. The totals swing from year to year mainly because the number of reporting kabupaten/kota changes (307 in 2024 vs 278 on the 2025 dashboard). SIPSN is therefore a self-reported, incomplete sample of Indonesia's 514 kabupaten/kota, not a census.

### Cited Findings
**Timbulan & % terkelola, 2025 [konflik: four different totals for the same year]:**
- SIPSN dashboard: about 29.2 million tonnes/year from **278 kabupaten/kota** reporting for 2025. Managed 32.9% (about 9.6 Mt); unmanaged about 19.6 Mt — [SIPSN](https://sipsn.menlhk.go.id/sipsn/) (via search summary; the dashboard is live and changes as regions input data)
- 24.8 Mt; only 34.55% (about 8.5 Mt) "terkelola dengan baik", about 16.3 Mt not handled. Headline: "65 persen sampah nasional belum terkelola" — [Kompas Lestari, 26 Feb 2026](https://lestari.kompas.com/read/2026/02/26/150034186/perlu-strategi-terpadu-atasi-65-persen-sampah-nasional-yang-belum-terkelola); same figure in [Belantara Foundation](https://www.belantara.or.id/berita-terkait/belantara-foundation/618/perlu-strategi-terpadu-atasi-65-persen-sampah-nasional-yang-belum-terkelola)
- 23.84 Mt timbulan; handled 9.69 Mt/year = 32.3%; "Pengelolaan sampah baru 34,7%, target RPJMN 2026 kian sulit tercapai" — [Datasatu](https://datasatu.com/ekonomi/2819961/pengelolaan-sampah-baru-347-target-rpjmn-2026-kian-sulit-tercapai)
- 25.14 Mt (SIPSN KLH 2025); household sector 56.7% of the total; about 34.49% managed well — [Mongabay Indonesia (Threads)](https://www.threads.com/@mongabayindonesia/post/DarixXKkdNW/berdasarkan-data-sistem-informasi-pengelolaan-sampah-nasional-sipsn-kementerian/); [GoodStats](https://goodstats.id/article/persentase-sumber-sampah-indonesia-2025-rumah-tangga-dominasi-timbulan-nasional-GR9A5)
- 2024: 33.34 Mt timbulan reported by **307 kabupaten/kota** (SIPSN) — cited in the search summary for [Antara Megapolitan](https://megapolitan.antaranews.com/berita/373321/menteri-lh-sebut-sisa-makanan-masih-jadi-jenis-timbulan-sampah-terbesar) and [Metro TV "34,54 Juta Ton Sampah Belum Terkelola Baik"](https://www.metrotvnews.com/read/NxGCGQdo-3454-juta-ton-sampah-belum-terkelola-baik). The exact attribution is unverified.
- Minister Hanif (March 2025) acknowledged that national waste management stood at **39.01%** against a target of 100% by 2029, and that the target would not be met that year — [Validnews](https://validnews.id/nasional/pemerintah-akan-tutup-tpa-open-dumping) / [JawaPos](https://www.jawapos.com/nasional/015708473/menteri-lingkungan-hidup-umumkan-penghentian-open-dumping-di-343-tpa) (via search summary). [konflik] 39.01% (2024 basis?) vs about 33–35% (2025 basis).

**Komposisi (SIPSN 2024) [small decimal conflicts]:**
- Food waste (sisa makanan): 39.36% / 39.63% / 39.87% depending on the source — [GoodStats "39% Sampah Indonesia adalah Sisa Makanan"](https://data.goodstats.id/statistic/39-sampah-indonesia-adalah-sisa-makanan-r1vK4); [Antara](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/4735177/klh-ingatkan-masyarakat-habiskan-makanan-guna-kurangi-food-waste); [Kompas Lestari, Apr 2026](https://lestari.kompas.com/read/2026/04/11/130300186/klh-dorong-pemda-untuk-bereskan-setengah-sampah-dari-rumah)
- Plastic: 19.32% to 19.64%, the second-largest component — same sources as above (Antara gives 19.54%)
- 2025: food waste is still dominant — [GoodStats "Sampah Indonesia Didominasi Sisa Makanan pada 2025"](https://goodstats.id/article/sampah-indonesia-didominasi-sisa-makanan-pada-2025-Yttj3)
- In some provinces (Kaltara to Yogyakarta) food waste is about half of timbulan — [Kumparan](https://kumparan.com/kumparannews/setengah-timbulan-sampah-di-kaltara-hingga-yogyakarta-adalah-sisa-makanan-23mDJiyo5B6)
- Regional example: SIPSN recorded 26.85% of waste in Kalimantan Selatan still dumped at open-dumping TPAs (May 2025) — [Kompas Lestari](https://lestari.kompas.com/read/2025/05/23/174200586/menteri-lh--pemprov-kalsel-baru-kelola-48-5-persen-sampah-setengahnya)

**Plastic-specific estimates (non-SIPSN) [estimate, older data]:**
- NPAP: Indonesia generates **6.8 Mt of plastic waste/year**, growing about 5%/year — [Global Plastic Action Partnership – Indonesia](https://www.globalplasticaction.org/indonesia) (baseline 2017)
- NPAP (via Wilson Center): only about **10%** of the 6.8 Mt is recycled, and almost half is dumped or burned — [Wilson Center](https://wilsoncenter.org/event/frontline-indonesias-plastic-waste-crisis)
- 61% of plastic waste was **not collected** in 2017 — [Statista](https://statista.com/statistics/1124941/indonesia-share-of-plastic-waste-collection-methods). Note: "uncollected" is not the same as "unmanaged".

### Inferences
- The "about 35% managed / about 65% unmanaged" framing for 2025 is consistent across sources even though the totals differ. The absolute tonnage is unreliable because only 278–307 of 514 kabupaten/kota report, and they report different years. The jump from 33.34 Mt (2024, 307 regions) to about 25 Mt (2025) almost certainly reflects reporting coverage, not a real drop in waste.
- Generation per capita implied by SIPSN (about 25–33 Mt for about 280 million people, or about 0.25–0.3 kg/person/day) looks low against international benchmarks, which suggests undercounting. Treat this as an inference, not a sourced claim.
- Because food waste is the largest stream (about 40%), the methane and landfill-fire problem (Q4) is driven by organic waste. Packaging plastic (about 19–20%) drives river and sea leakage and the burning of mixed waste. The causal weight of each actor differs by stream: the government holds most leverage over organics (source separation, composting); producers hold it over packaging.

### Gaps
- I could not open the SIPSN dashboard directly to verify current 2025/2026 figures, the composition breakdown for 2025, or the national share of waste going to open-dumping TPAs.
- No official 2026 (year-to-date) data found.
- No independent audit of SIPSN methodology found. Data-quality critiques by Bappenas or the World Bank were not retrieved in this pass.

## Q2. Siapa korporasi/merek dominan dalam polusi kemasan plastik (brand audit) dan argumen ekonomi sachet

### Takeaway
Sungai Watch's 2024 audit (623,021 items, Bali and Banyuwangi) ranked **Wings** first (52,600 items), ahead of Danone, Indofood, Unilever, Mayora, Ultrajaya, OT, Nestlé, Yakult and Sosro. At least 41% of Wings items were sachets that cannot currently be recycled in Indonesia. Companies and industry groups dispute whether the audit is representative. The Environment Minister has said he will hold brands accountable.

### Cited Findings
- **Sungai Watch Brand Audit 2024** (published Feb 2025), top 10 parent companies by item count: 1 Wings 52,600; 2 Danone 39,480; 3 Indofood 34,043; 4 Unilever 32,372; 5 Mayora 30,209; 6 Ultrajaya 29,543; 7 OT 24,531; 8 Nestlé 17,678; 9 Yakult 17,243; 10 Sosro 16,419. Total sample 623,021 items from Banyuwangi and Bali — [Liputan6](https://www.liputan6.com/lifestyle/read/5936882/10-perusahaan-penyumbang-sampah-plastik-terbanyak-pada-2024-menurut-sungai-watch)
- At least **41% of Wings' plastic waste** came from single-use sachets "yang saat ini tidak dapat didaur ulang di Indonesia". Sungai Watch: "Kesenjangan antara kebijakan dan aksi masih lebar" — [Liputan6](https://www.liputan6.com/lifestyle/read/5936882/10-perusahaan-penyumbang-sampah-plastik-terbanyak-pada-2024-menurut-sungai-watch); see also [Kompas Money](https://money.kompas.com/read/2025/02/28/145043826/sungai-watch-soroti-merek-yang-jadi-penyumbang-sampah-di-sungai-produsen-amdk)
- **2023 audit:** Danone's Aqua was the most common single *brand* found in rivers — [Bioplastics News, Feb 2024](https://bioplasticsnews.com/2024/02/20/b-corp-certified-bottled-water-brand-aqua-tops-audit-of-indonesias-biggest-plastic-polluters/). In 2024, Sungai Watch said "for the first time in four years, we have a new leader among the top polluting corporations" (Danone fell to second) — [The Bali Media](https://thebalimedia.com/sungai-watch-new-report-reveals-top-10-brand-contributors-to-balis-mounting-plastic-waste-but-is-the-problem-bigger-than-corporate-brands/)
- **Industry rebuttals:** Danone-Aqua called the audit "not representative" because it covers only areas with Sungai Watch river barriers — [Eco-Business](https://www.eco-business.com/news/river-trash-audit-not-representative-of-indonesias-waste-conditions-danone-aqua-responds/). Yaksindo called it "misleading and incomplete" for focusing on downstream surface river waste rather than full lifecycle and recyclability — [Jakarta Globe](https://jakartaglobe.id/news/yaksindo-slams-sungai-watchs-plastic-waste-audit-as-misleading-and-incomplete); [Antara Kupang](https://kupang.antaranews.com/berita/126507/yaksindo-slams-sungai-watchs-plastic-waste-audit-as-misleading-and-incomplete). A BRUIN researcher argued that a representative study would need to cover at least 51% of 38 provinces — [Sinar Harapan](https://www.sinarharapan.net/audit-sampah-sungai-watch-tidak-merepresentasikan-kondisi-di-indonesia/)
- **Government:** "Menteri LH Bakal Tuntut Tanggung Jawab Brand yang Sampah Plastiknya Cemari Lingkungan" — [Liputan6](https://www.liputan6.com/lifestyle/read/5981620/menteri-lh-bakal-tuntut-tanggung-jawab-brand-yang-sampah-plastiknya-cemari-lingkungan) (details of the mechanism not retrieved)
- **Who controls this stage:** packaging format (sachet, multilayer, PET bottle) is a producer decision. The state's lever is producer responsibility (the EPR/roadmap regulation, Permen LHK P.75/2019, is the known instrument). Not re-verified in this pass, see Gaps.

### Inferences
- The top polluters are mostly **domestic FMCG conglomerates** (Wings, Indofood, Mayora, OT, Ultrajaya, Sosro), not only multinationals (Danone, Unilever, Nestlé). Framing EPR as a "foreign brand" issue would miss most of the volume.
- Sachet argument: sachets sell small portions to low-income consumers, but multilayer and metallised sachets have no economic value for pemulung. They are not collected by the informal recycling chain and become the residual fraction that is burned, dumped or leaks to rivers (the Sungai Watch 41% figure supports this). The "affordability" defence is the producers' framing. The "externalised cost" critique comes from NGOs (BFFP, AZWI). I did not retrieve quotes for either side in this pass.
- Brand audits measure *visible, identifiable* items in specific catchments. They are evidence of attribution, not a national mass balance. Both the audits and the industry rebuttals have merit on representativeness.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved: the Break Free From Plastic global brand audit Indonesia results, the ECOTON brand audits (Brantas/Surabaya) and the AZWI audits. The search budget ran out.
- No data found on the national sachet volume (units/year) or on the multilayer share of plastic packaging.
- Status of producer roadmaps under Permen LHK P.75/2019, and whether KLH has sanctioned any producer, is unverified.

## Q3. Berapa plastik yang benar-benar didaur ulang; peran pemulung/sektor informal

### Takeaway
Only about 10% (NPAP) to about 15% of plastic waste is recycled. The recycling that exists depends heavily on an informal workforce estimated at 3.3–3.7 million people, possibly 5 million. These are 2017–2020 estimates, and no recent official figure was found.

### Cited Findings
- NPAP: about **10%** of 6.8 Mt/year of plastic is recycled; almost half is dumped or burned — [Wilson Center](https://wilsoncenter.org/event/frontline-indonesias-plastic-waste-crisis) [estimate, 2017 baseline]
- Another (NGO) source: only about **15%** of plastic is recycled — [Kinetik](https://kinetik.or.id/id/rasanya-seperti-keluarga-bagaimana-rezycology-meningkatkan-kehidupan-para-pemulung-sampah-penyandang-disabilitas/) [estimate, weak source]
- Government (via Antara): the recycling industry processes only about one-third of national waste capacity/needs — [Antara English](https://en.antaranews.com/news/177746/recycling-industry-processing-just-one-third-of-nations-waste-govt) (detail not fetched)
- Plastic recycling is dominated by the informal sector. The industry lacks feedstock, partly because some plastic waste is not recyclable — [Mongabay Indonesia, 2019 "Daur Ulang Sampah Plastik di Indonesia Rendah"](https://www.mongabay.co.id/2019/09/10/daur-ulang-sampah-plastik-di-indonesia-rendah/)
- **Pemulung:** KLHK cites about **3.7 million** informal workers/pemulung it wants integrated into formal systems — [Antara Kalteng](https://kalteng.antaranews.com/berita/370382/klhk-dorong-37-juta-pemulung-se-indonesia-masuk-ke-sektor-formal-pengolahan-sampah). IPI (Ikatan Pemulung Indonesia) counted 3.7 million in 25 provinces, possibly **5 million** with the 9 unsurveyed provinces (2019) — [CNN Indonesia](https://www.cnnindonesia.com/teknologi/20191119124619-199-449650/ada-37-juta-pemulung-klhk-diminta-tak-larang-plastik)
- The Ministry of Industry counts **3.3 million pemulung** as informal workers supporting the plastic recycling industry, alongside about 120,000 direct workers. 63,000 workers were laid off during COVID (2020) — [Tempo](https://www.tempo.co/ekonomi/industri-daur-ulang-plastik-rumahkan-63-ribu-buruh-akibat-corona-625389); [Republika](https://ekonomi.republika.co.id/berita/q9udwx383/industri-daur-ulang-plastik-rumahkan-63-ribu-pekerja)
- IPI has argued against plastic bans on the grounds of pemulung livelihoods (2019) — [CNN Indonesia](https://www.cnnindonesia.com/teknologi/20191119124619-199-449650/ada-37-juta-pemulung-klhk-diminta-tak-larang-plastik)

### Inferences
- The recycling chain is effectively **subsidised by unpaid or underpaid informal labour**. Producers benefit from the collection of high-value PET and HDPE without paying for it, while low-value plastics (sachets, multilayer, PS) are left out of the chain.
- Waste-to-energy and RDF facilities (Q4) can compete with pemulung for feedstock. This is a justice concern that NGOs (GAIA, AZWI) typically raise; I did not retrieve their statements here.

### Gaps
- No recent (2023–2026) national recycling rate by polymer found. No data on the share of sachets or multilayer in the residual stream.
- No sex-disaggregated data on waste pickers found (the women and gender angle remains unsourced).

## Q4. Status TPA: open dumping, krisis besar, kebakaran, perintah penutupan; RDF dan PSEL

### Takeaway
In 2025, KLH under Minister Hanif Faisol Nurofiq imposed administrative sanctions (orders to stop open dumping within about 180 days) on **343 TPA**, invoking Law 18/2008, which had required closure of open-dumping sites by 2013. Implementation has slipped: Suwung's closure was postponed from 23 December 2025 to 28 February 2026. Meanwhile mega-sites are at or past capacity (Bantargebang above 50 m high, about 7,350 t/day), and 2023 saw a wave of landfill fires led by Sarimukti. The government's main downstream bet is centralised waste-to-energy (Perpres 109/2025, PSEL run through Danantara) plus RDF.

### Cited Findings
**Open-dumping enforcement:**
- On 27 Feb 2025, before DPR Komisi XII, Hanif cited UU 18/2008 (Articles 44–45: local governments had to close open dumping within 5 years). KLH issued Circular SE.14/2025 and sent warnings to 306 regional heads — [Validnews](https://validnews.id/nasional/pemerintah-akan-tutup-tpa-open-dumping); [JawaPos](https://www.jawapos.com/nasional/015708473/menteri-lingkungan-hidup-umumkan-penghentian-open-dumping-di-343-tpa)
- Sanctions take two forms: ending open-dumping practice, or total permanent closure. Operators get 6 months to stop, then must build sanitary landfill zones and rehabilitate the site — [Detik Jateng](https://www.detik.com/jateng/berita/d-7874897/menteri-lh-tutup-sementara-343-tpa-open-dumping); [JawaPos](https://www.jawapos.com/kabinet-merah-putih/015748245/menteri-lhhanif-faisol-nurofiq-hentikan-343-tpametode-open-dumping)
- First batch: coercive sanctions on **37 TPA**. Final closure normally falls under the Public Works Ministry (PU) — [Tempo](https://www.tempo.co/lingkungan/klh-beri-sanksi-paksa-stop-open-dumping-37-tpa-di-indonesia-1217623). By April and May 2025, sanctions covered all 343 — [Antara](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/4779561/pemerintah-tutup-ratusan-pembuangan-sampah-terbuka-di-indonesia); [Antara video](https://www.antaranews.com/video/4831365/klh-beri-sanksi-343-tpa-untuk-optimalkan-penghentian-open-dumping)
- Note: 343 is the number of sanctioned TPA, not necessarily the total number of open-dumping TPA in Indonesia. No total national count was found.

**Suwung (Bali):**
- Since 1 Aug 2025, TPA Regional Sarbagita Suwung accepts no organic waste. It was due to close permanently at end-2025 (residue only), based on **Kepmen LHK No. 921/2025** (23 May 2025, 180 days to stop open dumping) — [Detik Bali](https://www.detik.com/bali/berita/d-8045955/tpa-suwung-tutup-permanen-akhir-2025-hanya-terima-sampah-residu); [Kabar Bali](https://kabarbali.id/akhir-2025-tpa-suwung-tutup-permanen-mulai-1-agustus-tak-terima-sampah-organik/)
- Closure scheduled for 23 Dec 2025 was cancelled; KLH extended the deadline to February 2026 (the province asked for 28 Feb 2026). Mongabay reports a "darurat sampah" in Bali and protests over the closure. Suwung was already over capacity — [Kompas Denpasar](https://denpasar.kompas.com/read/2025/12/23/102347278/tpa-suwung-bali-batal-ditutup-hari-ini-kementerian-lh-beri-waktu-sampai); [Mongabay Indonesia, 26 Dec 2025](https://mongabay.co.id/2025/12/26/darurat-sampah-di-bali-dan-protes-penutupan-tpa-suwung/)
- Site sizes (Mongabay methane study): Bantar Gebang about 79 ha, Sarimukti about 24 ha, Suwung about 23 ha — [Mongabay Indonesia](https://mongabay.co.id/2025/12/26/darurat-sampah-di-bali-dan-protes-penutupan-tpa-suwung/)

**Bantargebang & RDF Rorotan (Jakarta):**
- Bantargebang receives on average about **7,354 t/day** (peak 7,900). The pile is above **50 m** high (about a 16-storey building); some sources say nearly 60 m — [Kompas Lestari, Feb 2026](https://lestari.kompas.com/read/2026/02/06/102906386/rdf-rorotan-dinilai-efektif-kurangi-beban-sampah-jakarta)
- RDF Rorotan (built by WIKA, billed as the "largest in the world") has a design capacity of 2,500 t/day. It started operating in late December 2025 at 200 t/day, rising to 400–600 t/day, with a 1,000 t/day target. DLH says it will cut about one-third of Jakarta's waste going to Bantargebang. Even if everything runs optimally, about 3,500 t/day would still need landfilling — [Kompas Lestari](https://lestari.kompas.com/read/2025/09/23/173515586/dlh-rdf-rorotan-pangkas-sepertiga-sampah-jakarta-ke-bantargebang); [WIKA](https://www.wika.co.id/en/media-and-information/press-release/rdf-rorotan-karya-wika-rdf-waste-processing-pioneer-in-indonesia-largest-in-the-world)
- Residents of Jakarta Garden City (Cakung) demanded closure over odour and air pollution, saying no AMDAL had been shown to them. The facility is about 800 m from housing — [Kompas Lestari](https://lestari.kompas.com/read/2026/02/06/212119586/rdf-rorotan-bantu-kurangi-beban-bantargebang-ini-cerita-warga)
- Antara headline "A renewed vision for Jakarta after Bantargebang's waste breakdown" — [Antara English](https://en.antaranews.com/news/410446/a-renewed-vision-for-jakarta-after-bantargebangs-waste-breakdown) (content not fetched; the nature of the "breakdown" is unverified)

**Fires (2023):**
- The TPA Sarimukti (Bandung Barat) fire started around 19 Aug 2023 (sources say 18, 19 or 21 Aug). It burned about 19.5 ha (other sources say 15 ha). The TPA closed on 23 Aug and was declared fully extinguished on 25 Oct 2023. Methane from organic waste made it hard to put out. Jawa Barat declared a "Status Darurat Sampah Bandung Raya" (Kepgub 658/Kep.579-DLH/2023). 188 trucks were turned back, and waste piled up at TPS in Bandung and Cimahi — [Medcom](https://www.medcom.id/nasional/daerah/Obz0Ex7K-api-belum-padam-total-area-tpa-sarimukti-yang-terbakar-mencapai-19-5-hektare); [Detik Jabar](https://www.detik.com/jabar/berita/d-6986884/ditutup-selama-kebakaran-zona-1-dan-2-tpa-sarimukti-kini-kembali-dibuka); [Unpad journal](https://journals.unpad.ac.id/kumawula/article/download/62012/pdf); [Metro TV](https://www.metrotvnews.com/play/b2lCr28m-kebakaran-tpa-sarimukti-akibat-overload-dan-gas-metan)
- A DPR research brief (Sept 2023) compiled from media reports at least **13 other TPA/ex-TPA fires in 2023** besides Sarimukti, from TPA Terjun Medan (2 Apr 2023) to TPA Sente Klungkung (6 Sep 2023). The list is media-based and not exhaustive — [DPR Info Singkat XV-17 Sept 2023](https://berkas.dpr.go.id/pusaka/files/info_singkat/Info%20Singkat-XV-17-I-P3DI-September-2023-223.pdf)

**PSEL / waste-to-energy:**
- **Perpres 109/2025** replaces Perpres 35/2018, which was widely regarded as ineffective. Danantara leads: it prepares studies and selects the BUPP PSEL. Tariff is fixed at **USD 0.20/kWh for 30 years**, with PLN obligated to buy. Local governments must provide land and guarantee waste supply and transport. An early draft named 33 cities; the final text uses criteria instead of a list — [Ashurst](https://www.ashurstperkinscoie.com/en/insights/accelerating-waste-to-energy-in-indonesia-presidential-regulation-109-2025/); [KLH](https://kemenlh.go.id/news/detail/presiden-tetapkan-perpres-1092025-langkah-nyata-atasi-sampah-perkotaan); [ATD Mori Hamada](https://atdmorihamada.com/waste-to-energy-regulation-updated-a-significant-milestone-in-advancing-indonesias-circular-economy-and-clean-energy-transition-agenda/)
- Reported plan: about IDR 91 trillion investment across 33 cities, completion by end-2027; 107 business entities registered interest (late-2025 reporting) — [SUPRA International](https://supra-international.com/insights/indonesia-s-new-waste-to-energy-framework-presidential-regulation-109-2025-and-the-private-sector-response); [Business Indonesia](https://business-indonesia.org/news/danantara-to-launch-waste-to-energy-project-starting-next-month) [plan, not achievement]
- WALHI has a position paper on PSEL — [WALHI Kertas Posisi PSEL](https://walhi.or.id/uploads/WALHI%202025-2029/Dokumen/Kertas%20Posisi%20PSEL.pdf) (not opened; content unverified)

### Inferences
- **Who decides:** TPA operation is a local government responsibility (DLH kabupaten/kota). KLH holds the enforcement power (administrative sanctions). PU builds and closes landfills. Under Perpres 109/2025, decisions about thermal treatment move to the centre (Danantara and PLN), while local governments carry the supply-guarantee obligation. That obligation can create a "put-or-pay" style incentive against waste reduction and against pemulung access to feedstock. This is an inference; the contract terms were not verified.
- The closure orders without ready alternatives (Suwung) shift the crisis upstream to TPS, roadsides, rivers and open burning. This is the pattern seen in Bandung in 2023 and Bali in 2025–26.
- With about 40% organics, high landfill methane is the main driver of fires. Organic diversion at source would reduce fire risk more directly than incinerators.

### Gaps
- The total number of TPA in Indonesia and how many still practise open dumping (2025/26) was not retrieved. Compliance status of the 343 after the 180-day deadline is unknown.
- Suwung's status after Feb 2026, and Sarimukti's and Bantargebang's status in late 2026, were not verified.
- No data found on TPA fires in 2024–2025 beyond 2023, on progress of PSEL construction in 2026, or on GAIA and AZWI critiques of PSEL and RDF (search returned none).
- No national open-burning figure found. The NPAP "almost half dumped or burned" is the closest proxy.

## Q5. Kebocoran ke sungai/laut: angka dan sengketa metodologi (Jambeck dll.)

### Takeaway
Jambeck et al. (2015) estimated 0.48–1.29 Mt/year of Indonesian plastic entering the ocean (and ranked Indonesia second globally). The government, using a LIPI baseline, puts marine debris at 0.27–0.59 Mt/year. An ensemble of models gives about 0.52 ± 0.36 Mt/year. The ranking is politically contested, and model mismanagement estimates for Indonesia diverge widely.

### Cited Findings
- Jambeck et al. 2015: 3.22 Mt/year of mismanaged plastic waste in Indonesia, of which 0.48–1.29 Mt becomes ocean plastic. Studies from 2015 to 2019 estimated 0.27–1.29 Mt/year — [Duke Nicholas Institute, Plastic Pollution Policy Country Profile Indonesia](https://nicholasinstitute.duke.edu/sites/default/files/projects/Plastic-Pollution-Policy-Country-Profile-Indonesia.pdf)
- Government (2019): Indonesia produces **0.27–0.59 Mt of marine debris per year** (LIPI baseline). Some ministries were dissatisfied with Jambeck — [Jakarta Post, 12 Dec 2019](https://www.thejakartapost.com/news/2019/12/12/government-indonesia-produces-up-to-0-59-million-tons-of-marine-debris-per-year)
- Nurhati & Cordova (2020) compare Jambeck, Lebreton and World Bank (2018) models: ensemble mean **0.52 ± 0.36 Mt/year**. The national assessment used mass-flow analysis with secondary data from 514 kabupaten/kota — [BRIN e-journal](https://ejournal.brin.go.id/mri/article/download/14666/11327/42880)
- A Marine Pollution Bulletin 2021 paper (doi 10.1016/j.marpolbul.2021.112720) notes that Indonesia, along with China and Russia, shows large differences in mismanagement estimates between models — [FAO AGRIS record](https://agris.fao.org/search/zh/records/65df0f290f3e94b9e5d49090)
- NPAP (2020): Indonesia's plan aims to prevent 16 Mt of plastic from entering the ocean (to 2040, "net-zero plastic pollution by 2040") — [Eurasia Review](https://www.eurasiareview.com/?p=217374); [Indonesia Water Portal](https://www.indonesiawaterportal.com/tags/net-zero-pollution.html)

### Inferences
- Use ranges, not ranks. The headline "Indonesia #2 ocean polluter" rests on the 2010-data Jambeck model and is disputed. The government's lower figure is self-reported and also methodologically limited.
- Leakage is the downstream consequence of the roughly 65% unmanaged share (Q1) and of non-collectable sachets (Q2). Causal responsibility is shared between local governments (collection gaps) and producers (formats that are uneconomic to collect).

### Gaps
- The World Bank 2018 "Indonesia Marine Debris Hotspot Rapid Assessment" was not retrieved directly. Its hotspot figures (e.g., the share from rural and low-collection areas) are unverified here.
- No figures retrieved on Meijer et al. 2021 river-emission rankings for Indonesia, or on any post-2022 national leakage figure (KKMB/TKN-PSL progress toward a 70% reduction target by 2025).

## Q6. Impor sampah/kertas bekas dan dampak terdokumentasi (Bangun, Tropodo, dioksin)

### Takeaway
After China's National Sword policy (2018), Indonesia absorbed more paper and plastic scrap. One report cites BPS data of 3.24 Mt of paper scrap (+6%) and 252,472 t of plastic scrap (+30%) in a recent year (year unverified). ECOTON finds plastic contamination of up to 35% in imported paper, and 60–82% of Australian paper shipments unrecyclable, against a 2% legal cap. Plastic separated from imported paper was burned as fuel by tofu factories in Tropodo (Sidoarjo) and dumped in Bangun (Mojokerto). Free-range eggs there had dioxin levels of 200 and 10.8 pg TEQ/g fat respectively, against the BPOM limit of 2.5.

### Cited Findings
- In 2019 Indonesia denied entry to 58 US containers (Jul–Aug). The government denied agreeing to re-export, and re-export documents named other companies — [Mongabay, Nov 2019](https://news.mongabay.com/2019/11/indonesia-waste-plastic-export-import-illegal/). Critics say "re-export" often meant shipment on to other developing countries (Yuyun Ismawati, Nexus3) — [Pulitzer Center](https://pulitzercenter.org/stories/indonesia-cracks-down-scourge-imported-plastic-waste)
- About 450 containers had been re-exported by 2020 — [Trash Hero / study "Plastic Waste Trade in Indonesia and Country's Response to Waste Trade Challenges" (2024)](https://trashhero.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Plastic-Waste-Trade-in-Indonesia-and-Countrys-Response-to-Waste-Trade-Challenges.pdf)
- NGOs (2019) demanded a 0.5% contamination cap and an end to dirty plastic imports by end-2020, in line with the Basel COP-14 amendment. They said almost all monitored paper companies misused their import licences — [Break Free From Plastic, 29 Aug 2019](https://www.breakfreefromplastic.org/2019/08/29/environmental-activists-call-indonesian-government-plastic-waste-importation-contaminants/)
- BPS (cited by Asia News Network): paper scrap imports up 6% to **3.24 Mt**, plastic scrap up 30% to **252,472 t** ("last year"; exact year not verified). ECOTON: **60–82%** of Australian paper waste shipped to Indonesia was unrecyclable due to flexible plastic contamination and piled up at illegal dumps — [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/growing-paper-plastic-scrap-imports-aggravate-indonesias-waste-problem/)
- ECOTON: contamination of imported paper up to **35%**, far above the **2%** limit in the joint ministerial decree — [Heinrich Böll Stiftung, "Not Our Trash", Sept 2025](https://th.boell.org/en/2025/09/15/not-our-trash-indonesias-plastic-waste)
- **Dioxin egg study (IPEN, Nexus3, ECOTON, Arnika, Nov 2019):** Tropodo about **200 pg TEQ/g fat**, Bangun **10.8 pg TEQ/g fat**, both above the BPOM standard of 2.5. Tropodo is the second-highest in Asia (after Bien Hoa, Vietnam). Imported paper-waste plastic is used as tofu-factory fuel (Tropodo) and dumped and burned (Bangun) — [Republika](https://tekno.republika.co.id/berita/trendtek/sains-trendtek/19/11/14/q0yb4p414-telur-ayam-kampung-di-dua-desa-terkontaminasi-dioksin); [Kompas](https://www.kompas.com/tren/read/2019/11/19/165223865/menguak-laporan-penelitian-telur-dengan-kandungan-dioksin-tinggi?page=all); [AZWI press release](https://aliansizerowaste.id/2019/11/28/siaran-pers-dioksin-dari-dua-desa-ke-duabelas-kota/); peer-reviewed/preprint version: ["Plastic waste flooding Indonesia leads to toxic chemical contamination of the food chain"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/338225066_Plastic_waste_flooding_Indonesia_leads_to_toxic_chemical_contamination_of_the_food_chain)
- LIPI analysis of contaminated eggs and tofu in Tropodo — [Tempo](https://www.tempo.co/lingkungan/telur-dan-tahu-tercemar-dioksin-di-tropodo-begini-analisis-lipi-681854). Government response: eggs "bukan dari peternakan modern" — [VOA Indonesia](https://www.voaindonesia.com/a/isu-telur-mengandung-dioksin-pemerintah-pastikan-bukan-dari-peternakan-modern/5188957.html)
- Kompas investigation "Telur Berdioksin dari Dollar Sampah" — [Kompas.id](https://www.kompas.id/artikel/telur-berdioksin-dari-dollar-sampah)
- Follow-up: Nexus3 and Arnika's "Toxic Hotspots in Java II" (2024) again lists Tropodo as affected by plastic-as-fuel. A local outlet ran a 2026 follow-up: "Telur Desa Tropodo Sidoarjo Beracun Tertinggi Kedua di Asia" — [Jatim Times, 7 Oct 2026](https://jatimtimes.com/baca/3331351525/20261007/113400/telur-desa-tropodo-sidoarjo-beracun-tertinggi-kedua-di-asia-ini-penyebabnya)
- Overview of the waste trade — [Yale e360](https://e360.yale.edu/features/plastic-waste-imports-recycling-indonesia)

### Inferences
- **Causal chain:** Exporting-country municipal systems (US, Australia, EU) export low-grade mixed paper. Indonesian paper mills (licensed importers) accept contaminated bales. The plastic reject is sold or given to villages, which earn income from sorting it. Low-value residue is burned as fuel (tofu factories) or burned in the open, and dioxins enter the food chain. Decision power sits with paper mills (import specs), the Trade and Environment ministries (licences, the contamination cap) and customs (inspection). The exposure falls on villagers.
- The 2% cap (vs 0.5% demanded by NGOs) and the 35% contamination found suggest weak enforcement. This is inferred from the gap; I found no enforcement data.

### Gaps
- Not found: verified BPS import volumes for 2023–2025 by HS code (4707 paper, 3915 plastic), the current status of the contamination regulation (whether 2% is still in force or was revised), or any 2025–2026 re-export or ban actions.
- No recent dioxin re-testing data besides "Toxic Hotspots in Java II" (2024), and its figures were not retrieved.

## Q7. Dimensi kesehatan & keadilan: siapa menanggung beban

### Takeaway
The documented burden falls on communities living next to dumpsites and processing sites (Tropodo and Bangun dioxin exposure; Sarimukti fire and smoke; Rorotan odour complaints; Bali and Bandung waste emergencies after closures) and on 3.3–5 million informal waste pickers. Decisions are made by producers (packaging), importers (paper mills) and central and local governments (TPA, PSEL siting). Gender-specific data was not found in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- Dioxin in eggs at 4–80 times the BPOM limit in villages processing imported waste — [Republika](https://tekno.republika.co.id/berita/trendtek/sains-trendtek/19/11/14/q0yb4p414-telur-ayam-kampung-di-dua-desa-terkontaminasi-dioksin) (ratio computed from 10.8 and 200 vs 2.5)
- Residents near the RDF Rorotan facility (800 m away) complain of odour and air pollution and lack of AMDAL disclosure — [Kompas Lestari](https://lestari.kompas.com/read/2026/02/06/212119586/rdf-rorotan-bantu-kurangi-beban-bantargebang-ini-cerita-warga)
- BAZNAS Jabar distributed 3,000 food packages to residents affected by the Sarimukti fire (2023), showing that community impacts were recognised — [BAZNAS Jabar](https://www.baznasjabar.org/news/baznas_jawa_barat_berbagi_3000_sembako_untuk_warga_terdampak_kebakaran_tpa_sarimukti)
- Initial suspicion fell on pemulung for the Sarimukti fire (a cigarette butt), a framing that blames workers rather than an overloaded, methane-rich site — [DPR Info Singkat Sept 2023](https://berkas.dpr.go.id/pusaka/files/info_singkat/Info%20Singkat-XV-17-I-P3DI-September-2023-223.pdf); [Metro TV](https://www.metrotvnews.com/play/b2lCr28m-kebakaran-tpa-sarimukti-akibat-overload-dan-gas-metan)
- Pemulung livelihoods are precarious: 63,000 recycling workers were laid off in 2020 — [Tempo](https://www.tempo.co/ekonomi/industri-daur-ulang-plastik-rumahkan-63-ribu-buruh-akibat-corona-625389)

### Inferences
- **Accountability asymmetry:** the companies that design non-recyclable packaging and the importers that accept contaminated bales capture the economic benefit. The costs are borne by local budgets (APBD for collection and TPA), informal workers and fenceline communities.
- Closing TPA without alternatives redistributes burden to poorer neighbourhoods and rivers rather than reducing it.

### Gaps
- No sex-disaggregated or women-specific health data found (e.g., women sorters in Bangun, women pemulung at Bantargebang).
- No epidemiological studies retrieved on respiratory or other health outcomes near Bantargebang, Sarimukti or Suwung.
- Not retrieved: GAIA Asia Pacific and AZWI justice analyses of PSEL and RDF, or the incinerator dioxin monitoring debate.
